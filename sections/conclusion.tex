\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

At equal parameter count within the \texttt{lora\_B} pool, the random mask achieves the lower loss-based BWT in every seed, an advantage that reproduces on a second GPU and is not an artifact of density; the density-unmatched within-\texttt{lora\_B} comparison points the same way, and the zero-initialization confound explains why earlier comparisons that did not match the pool failed to see it. The practical consequence follows from the cost structure this removes: the attribution pass answers a question a deployment setting re-asks whenever the task stream changes, namely which parameters should absorb the new task, and our measurement says the pass is not needed to answer it. Dropping it removes a per-task backward pass over the calibration set, 12.8\% of the training budget in our configuration, and releases the calibration set with its activations, since nothing is scored. All comparisons are loss-based and scoped to the tested task order; a per-task adaptive mask or a non-NF4 quantization would be the most informative next test.
