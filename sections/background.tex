\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\textbf{Continual learning (CL) and Catastrophic Forgetting:} ~\citep{mccloskey1989catastrophic, french1999catastrophic}. A dominant strategy identifies important parameters and protects them during later tasks: EWC~\citep{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} uses the diagonal Fisher information as a quadratic penalty, SI~\citep{zenke2017continual} accumulates path-integral importance, and gradient-based selective-update methods score parameters from calibration gradients, an idea since extended to LLMs and parameter-efficient tuning~\citep{qiao2024pegp, wang2025gorp}. Its cost is the attribution pass's transient backward peak, which a random within-\texttt{lora\_B} mask of this work removes entirely (Appendix~\ref{app:attribution_cost}).

\textbf{Random Subspaces and Intrinsic Dimensionality:} The alternative view is that the identity of the selected parameters matters less than how many are selected. The lottery ticket hypothesis~\citep{frankle2019lottery} and supermasks~\citep{zhou2019deconstructing, ramanujan2020whats} show random subnetworks in large networks can perform strongly, \citet{aghajanyan2020intrinsic} and \citet{li2018measuring} show that fine-tuning occupies a low-dimensional subspace, and DARE~\citep{yu2024dare} shows that most delta parameters can be dropped. The same theme appears in pruning, where \citet{liu2019rethinking} show pruned architectures can be retrained from scratch and \citet{blalock2020state} find that method choice matters less than the sparsity budget. LoRA already constrains updates to a low-rank subspace and masking restricts further within it, so the question is whether attribution beats a random restriction of the same size.
